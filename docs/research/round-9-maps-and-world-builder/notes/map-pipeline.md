# Map pipeline: how each map layer is made, from country to street and interiors

Status: 6 October 2026, round 9, question 1. All five sub-questions are answered below.

Source labels follow `.claude/rules/docs.md`:
- "opened": read in full;
- "search summary": seen only in a search result;
- "measured here": run by me;
- "computed": derived by me;
- "inference" and "unsourced estimate".

Repo files are cited by path, and I opened all of them. Every prototype named here is in `../prototypes/pipeline/`.

Machine for every timing: a Windows 10 Pro 10.0.19045 desktop with an AMD Ryzen 5 3600, running Node v24.18.0, headless Chromium 153 and Python 3.14.6. No timing here is a phone timing.

**In brief**
- **Pipeline:**
  - one seed builds the country on a square grid, the TS port of `tools/worldgen`;
  - the Region view draws the same cells at 16 px;
  - each settlement gets a plan, then districts generated when they come into view, with code-drawn sprite kits;
  - hero towns are generated, then hand-edited;
  - interiors stay abstract;
  - routes become strip maps built by the same place code.
- **Mesh or grid:** the grid. Drop round 4's Voronoi mesh, and re-baseline M8 for 40–61 listed settlements plus a region tier.
- **Port check:** zero mismatches in Node 24.18 and Chromium 153. This covered 1,132,769 kernel cases (draws, `below`, noise, `fade`) and 300 of 300 terrain-stage fingerprints. The JS terrain stage runs about 90–100× faster than Python.
- **Art:** about 55–81 small tiles unblock every layer. They cover map-scale coast overlays, snow, cliffs facing every way, rock and sand variety. Rivers and roads can stay pixel lines.

## Q1. Layers: what to generate, what to hand-make, and one pipeline

### Takeaway

- **Every layer is generated from the seed.** Country and Region come from one grid; then a settlement plan, then districts, then abstract interiors. Routes become strip maps built by the same place code. Hand work enters only as kits (sprites, small block templates) and as edits on generated places: hero towns and the default town.
- **tools/worldgen replaces three planned methods:** round 4's LDtk village kits, the BSP-plus-LDtk-prefab town generator, and round 3's wave function collapse (WFC) filler. Its code-built villages and towns already show the GBA-era street grammar from a small context record (renders, measured here).
- **It does not yet build sim maps.** A place is one view of 48×28, 40×24 or 32×20 tiles, taking 0.1–1.6 s in Python. It ignores population and exports no walkability or zone entities. M3 needs a 256×256 town for 10,000 agents, and M6 needs cities of 400² to 1,024².
- **21 conflicts and gaps** are listed below. The largest are mesh against grid (Q2), 40–61 listed settlements against 1,000, and place size and output. Others are two keyed-draw definitions and missing sea lanes. Round 4's wealth-keyed prefab variants are now banned by content rule 5.
- **Other games split layers the same way.** Cataclysm: DDA (CDDA) and Minecraft assemble hand-made pieces on procedural ground. Dwarf Fortress generates every layer. Caves of Qud and Pokémon hand-make their hero places. Azgaar's FMG passes each settlement a seed plus a small context record.

### Cited Findings

**How other games split the layers**
- **Dwarf Fortress: every layer procedural, generated lazily.**
  - Blocks are 16×16 local tiles, and region tiles are 48×48 local tiles.
  - World tiles are 16×16 region tiles, or 768×768 local tiles. — [DFHack Maps.rst](https://github.com/DFHack/dfhack/blob/develop/docs/api/Maps.rst) (opened).
  - Region tiles "are only generated by DF as you scroll around on the zoomed-in embark map". — [DFHack export-world-map.rst](https://github.com/DFHack/dfhack/blob/develop/docs/plugins/export-world-map.rst) (opened).
- **Caves of Qud: hand-made overworld and hero villages, procedural zones and villages.**
  - The world map's "overall appearance is largely the same in every game session". It measures 80×25 parasangs, and each parasang is "a 3x3 area of 9 zones", where zones are the screens you play in. — [Qud wiki, World map](https://wiki.cavesofqud.com/wiki/World_map) (opened).
  - Five static villages appear in every playthrough. Generated villages are added per biome (5–8 in the jungle), each with two generated history events. — [Qud wiki, Village](https://wiki.cavesofqud.com/wiki/Village) (opened, as of patch 2.0.209.40).
- **CDDA: procedural overworld, hand-authored building layouts picked at random.**
  - The overmap is 180×180 terrains wide and 21 deep. It generates rivers, lakes, forests, swamps, ravines, cities, trails, roads and then specials.
  - It knows "where the cities are, where the roads are, where the buildings are, what types the buildings are". It does not know "what the actual building layout looks like". A defined city list suppresses random cities.
  - Shared decisions are "Resolved lazily when the first OMT reaches mapgen" and then persisted. — [CDDA OVERMAP.md](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/master/doc/JSON/OVERMAP.md) (opened).
  - On approach, "the game chooses semi-randomly from a list of functions for 'house'". "All mapgen functions build in a 24x24 tile area." Layouts are authored as JSON rows with palettes, weights (default 1,000) and nested chunks.
  - `place_nested` checks overmap neighbours, which "is useful for creating smoother transitions between biome types". — [CDDA MAPGEN.md](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/master/doc/JSON/MAPGEN.md) (opened).
- **FMG and Watabou: one generator per layer, joined by a seed and a context record.**
  - Each burg's seed is `burg.MFCG || options.map.seed + String(burg.i).padStart(4, "0")`, so a stored seed can override it.
  - The city generator gets name, population, seed and size (2.13 × (population ÷ density)^0.385, clamped to 6–100). Its flags are river, coast, farms, citadel, urban castle, hub (crossroad), plaza, greens, temple, walls, shantytown, style and sea direction.
  - The village generator gets population, seed, a width of 400–1,600 by population, and a sand, snow or default style. Its tags include estuary, river, highway, dead end, farmland, no square, palisade and sparse or dense.
  - A third link opens Watabou's Dwellings, a separate interior generator keyed by the same burg seed. — [FMG burgs-generator.ts](https://github.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/blob/master/src/generators/burgs-generator.ts) (opened).
- **Pokémon Emerald: every map hand-made; interiors are separate small maps reached by warps.** This uses pret data for numbers only. Emerald has 518 maps in 34 groups. Littleroot Town has 3 warps (two houses and a lab) and 1 edge connection, up to Route 101. Of 441 layouts, 170 look like interiors, with a median of 182 tiles; 10×9 is the commonest size. — [pokeemerald map.json, layouts.json, map_groups.json](https://github.com/pret/pokeemerald/tree/master/data) (opened; my tally). Towns are 20×20 tiles and cities up to 80×40 (round 4, opened there).
- **Minecraft: villages assembled from hand-made pieces.** "Jigsaw structures are used for the generation of pillager outposts, villages, bastion remnants, ancient cities, trail ruins, and trial chambers." They are built "out of smaller templates", up to 20 levels deep. — [Minecraft Wiki, Jigsaw block](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Jigsaw_block) (opened).

- **Round 3:** hand-author the 256² default town in LDtk, and "generate larger cities from a seeded road grid stamped with hand-made prefab blocks". "Use wave function collapse only for decorative filler." Ten thousand agents need a 256×256 town, 25,000 need about 400², and 100,000 need 800²–1,024². — [round 3 report](../../round-3-2d-look/report.md) (opened).

  - cities of 256² and above, with "prefab variants … keyed to district wealth and crime".

- **Plan.**
  - M3 hand-authors `town.ldtk` with IntGrid walkability and entities "with capacity, owner and opening hours".
  - M6 generates cities "from a seeded road grid stamped with LDtk prefab blocks".

  - M9 builds settlement "interiors by tier": the zoomed-in street maps, from LDtk village kits, BSP towns and M6's cities. It also builds route strips 20–40 tiles wide. No task builds walk-in building interiors. — `docs/plan/implementation-plan.md` (opened).
- **Content rule 5:** "Wealth never shows in bodies, clothes or houses by default." The sprites README adds "no ruined or patched variants". — `.claude/rules/content.md`; `tools/sprites/README.md` (opened).

**What tools/worldgen does now** (all opened)
- **Place sizes and inputs.**
  - Places are 48×28 (capital and city), 40×24 (town) or 32×20 (village and hamlet) tiles, and wonder views 30×18.
  - House counts come from tier ranges (3–5 in a hamlet, 20–30 in a capital), never from `ctx.population`, which `place.py` never reads.
  - Layout runs in a fixed order: ground, sea and rivers, roads and plaza, civic, houses, landmarks and wonder, trees, fields and animals, then people.
  - `Site` records doors and footprints, but `Layout` exports only terrain kinds, tiles, sprites and people. — `tools/worldgen/place.py`, `README.md`.
- **Country routes:** the reference builds them as cell paths. — `tools/worldgen/roads.py`.
- **Interiors:** there is no interior tile, map or generator. — `assets/sprites/*.json`.

**Per-layer decision**

| Layer | How it is made | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Country | Fully procedural: the TS port of tools/worldgen on the grid | Seeds must be shareable. The country takes 1.66 s in Python, an estimated 15–35 ms in JS |
| Region | The same cells drawn at 16 px | One cell per tile at both zooms; no second generator to keep consistent |
| Settlement plan | Fully procedural from the place record: arterials from road sides, plaza, district zoning | Cheap, and it feeds the City-zoom minimap without building every lot |
| District and street | Procedural layout stamped with hand-made kits (sprites, small block templates). Hero places are generated, then hand-edited | `place.py` proves the grammar, kits keep the art direction, and edits give curated towns |
| Interior | Abstract: building cards with occupants and stock pips. Later, optionally, one hand-made interior kit per building kind with keyed dressing | No interior art exists. The sim needs only "indoors", and Pokémon reuses small interiors |
| Route | Fully procedural strip maps chained from the route's cells, built with the place code | Matches the Region view, and the code already handles river crossings, bridges and biomes |

**One pipeline**
1. **Country.** The world seed runs the TS port of the country stages. The arrays are stored in the save as columnar data, with per-stage fingerprints. Country and Region tilemaps draw the same cells.
2. **Ledgers.** Listed settlements and wonders get ledgers and a place record (Q3). Unlisted ranks go to region accounts. Route ledgers come from road paths plus sea lanes.
3. **Settlement plan.** On first focus, a plan is built from the place seed and the static record. It lays out entries from road sides, rivers, coast, a plaza and arterials. It zones districts as core, residential, works, docks or fields. Its size comes from population, as round 3 sized towns. The plan is drawn as the City-zoom minimap.
4. **Districts.** Each district of about 64×64 tiles is generated when it enters view. The key is (place seed, district x, district y), plus its zone and the plan's roads at its edges.
   - The core district reuses `place.py`'s plaza, civic and landmark logic.
   - Outer districts pack lots along block frontage, as round 4's BSP prototype did, using the sprite catalogue and block templates.
5. **Output.** One binary map holds terrain kinds and an IntGrid for walkability, plus entities: homes with capacity, workplaces by sector, shops with hours, and civic buildings. Generated and hand-made maps share this format. M2 and M9's spawner reads it.
6. **Edits.** Hero towns and builder changes are stored as diffs keyed by place, district and generator version. LDtk becomes optional, for authoring kits and interop, not the town's source.
7. **Routes and interiors.** Route strips come from the route's cell sequence. Interiors stay abstract.

**Why the place code must be restructured** (computed)
- A 256² town has 49× the cells of a 48×28 capital, and hundreds to thousands of lots instead of 20–60.
- `find_lot` tests every footprint position and runs a BFS for its door path, so its cost grows with cells × lots. At 256², that is minutes in Python and seconds even at the 90–100× JS speed-up.
- Block-local frontage packing (round 4: 256² in 59 ms) and per-district laziness are therefore required before M3 or M6 can use it.

**Plan changes**
- **M0.** Make worldgen's `draw(seed, stream, ...keys)` the sim's one keyed draw, with fixed-arity hot-path variants. Load a binary map from either the generator or LDtk.
- **M3.**
  - The default town becomes a fixed seed generated by the TS place generator, then hand-edited. This pulls the TS port of `place.py` (1,737 lines), the district grid and entity export forward from M6. If that slips, a hand-made LDtk town is the stopgap, and M6 replaces it.
  - The Ninja Adventure re-download and re-index tasks shrink to placeholders.
- **M6.** Generated cities use the same generator at 400²–1,024² with lazy districts. Drop WFC.
- **M8.** Build on the grid (Q2). Add sea lanes, regions, names, and wonder and landmark icons.
- **M9.**
  - Replace "Build interiors by tier (LDtk village kits …, BSP towns …, cities from M6's generator)" with the district generator for every tier. This drops the 3–5 days of village-kit authoring.
  - Record a new decision: walk-in building interiors stay abstract, as building cards.
  - Build route strips from route cells.
  - Add wonder views, with the art in Q5.

**Pokémon-style towns.** Hand-made towns cannot follow a fresh seed per game, so Nomos borrows their grammar, as `place.py` does. It keeps hand-making for a few hero places: the tutorial village and the default town. This is Qud's split of static and generated villages, and CDDA's defined cities.

## Q2. Country representation: Voronoi mesh or square grid

### Takeaway

- **Use the square grid, and drop round 4's Voronoi mesh.** One grid cell is one 8-px Country tile and one 16-px Region tile. The mesh would have to be rasterised back into exactly this grid before any sprite could draw it.
- **Speed favours the grid.** The integer grid's terrain stage took about 8 ms in JS for 96×64 and matched Python bit for bit on 300 seeds (measured here). Round 4's mesh spent 116–145 ms of 510–587 ms on Delaunay and adjacency alone at 30,000 cells (round 4, measured there).
- **The art already fits the grid.** The existing 12-tile shore rule covers 99.6% of coast cells on reference countries (3,618 of 3,633). No new tile shape is needed, only map-scale versions of tiles the place view already has (measured here).
- **Settlement counts conflict with the plan.** A 96×64 world lists 40–61 settlements, not the plan's 1,000 on 30,000 cells. Growing the grid to 384×256 gave 938, but 904 of them were hamlets (measured here). Keep 96×64 as the standard world. The rank rule's unlisted places (56–63% of people, computed) become M7's region tier.
- **Plan changes:** rewrite M8's terrain, settlement and route tasks for the grid, and re-baseline its exit check. Make Region the same grid at 16 px. Test Zipf on true ranks. Add sea lanes between landmasses.

### Cited Findings

**Representation and cost**
- **What the reference uses.** It runs on a 96×64 grid (6,144 cells) indexed `y * width + x`. Neighbour lists keep one fixed order, "orthogonal steps first, so every stage visits neighbours the same way and a port can match it". — `tools/worldgen/grid.py`, `world.py` (opened).
- **What round 4 measured for the mesh.** A 1,000-settlement country took 587 ms on 29,929 cells, 116 ms of it Delaunay plus adjacency. With 300 settlements, that step took 145 of 510 ms. On 10,000 cells it took 216–227 ms, and on 99,856 cells 1,072–1,366 ms. All runs were cold Node 22 on a Xeon. — [round 4 world-maps.md, Q1](../../round-4-multi-scale/notes/world-maps.md) (opened; measured there).
- **Grid generation time, Python reference.**

  | Grid | Cells | Total time | Settlements | Routes | Road cells |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 96×64 | 6,144 | 1.66 s median [1.48–2.22] (10 seeds) | 40–61 | 57–115 | 365–557 |
  | 192×128 | 24,576 | 6.2 s (3 seeds) | 182–237 | 352–480 | 1,986–2,472 |
  | 384×256 | 98,304 | 28.1 s (1 seed) | 938 | 2,005 | 9,688 |

  Cost grows about linearly with cells — `stages.py` (measured here).
- **Grid generation time, JS port.** The terrain stage, the costliest Python stage at 46% of the total, took 8.6 ms warm in Node and 7.8 ms in Chromium at 96×64. A 5-octave fbm took 38 ms over 384×256 (measured here; environment in Q4).
- **Point location.** A grid finds the cell under a pixel with two shifts (`x >> 3`). Round 4 located mesh cells with `d3-delaunay` `find()` and a raster-order hint: 56 ms for a 512² raster, 210 ms for 1,024². — [round 4 world-maps.md, Q7](../../round-4-multi-scale/notes/world-maps.md) (opened; measured there).
- **Memory** (computed). The reference's per-cell columns are elevation, biome, temperature, moisture, river, coast and receiver. At 96×64 they take about 66 KB as Int16, Uint8 and Int32 arrays, and about 1.1 MB at 384×256, with no coordinate or adjacency arrays. Round 4's per-cell binary with quantised coordinates was 269 KB raw for 30,000 cells (round 4, measured there).

**Settlements and ledgers**
- **The rank rule.** "A country map shows only its notable places, so the k-th shown place has a true rank that grows faster than k: exactly P1/k for the top HEAD, then steeper." In the code, rank = k + (k − 6)²//2, with a capital of 150,000–500,000. — `tools/worldgen/settle.py` (opened).
- **Tier mix by grid size.** At 96×64 the 10 seeds gave 1 capital, 2–7 cities, 8–12 towns, 17–31 villages and 2–27 hamlets. At 192×128 they gave 1 capital, 2–4 cities, 9–10 towns, 17–23 villages and 153–203 hamlets. At 384×256 they gave 1 capital, 3 cities, 10 towns, 20 villages and 904 hamlets. Only hamlets multiply as land grows — `stages.py` (measured here).
- **Unlisted population** (computed, ignoring the ±25% size jitter). The 40–61 listed places reach true rank 618–1,573. They hold 37–44% of the population that P₁/r implies down to 200 people, which is 1.1–4.2 million. The other 56–63% lives in about 600–2,500 unlisted places.
- **Islands get no links.** Route pairs are made only "if a < b and mass[a] == mass[b]", so islands get separate road networks and no link between them. — `tools/worldgen/roads.py` (opened). Round 4 instead routed edges with no land path as sea lanes, 1–5% of edges (round 4, measured there).

**Art fit**
- **Coast cells by shore rule** (10 worlds, 61,440 cells; `mapcodes.py`; measured here). There were 3,633 land cells beside water, 807 of them on cliff coasts.

  | Shore case | Share |
  | --- | --- |
  | One wet side | 48.1% |
  | Outer corner | 25.4% |
  | Inner corner | 26.1% |
  | Not drawable (opposite or 3+ wet sides) | 0.4% |
- **Diagonal steps.** Of 1,188 river links, 66.5% are diagonal, and so are 40.1% of 7,586 road steps. A 4-way connection tile set can't draw them without extra steps (measured here).
- **How the views draw today.** Every Country and Region tile is a square sprite (`map8_*`, `map16_*`) chosen per cell, and settlement icons anchor to cells. Rivers and roads are drawn as palette-coloured pixel lines between cell centres. — `tools/worldgen/mapdraw.py`; `assets/sprites/map.json` (opened).
- **Autotile counts.** A corner set "with 2 terrains has 16 tiles", and an edge set, "common for roads, fences or platforms", also has 16. A mixed set has 256, "but reduced sets like the 47-tile Blob tileset" also work. — [Tiled manual, terrain.rst](https://github.com/mapeditor/tiled/blob/master/docs/manual/terrain.rst) (opened).

**Determinism**
- **The grid is proven.** The integer-only grid stage matched in V8-Node and Chromium on 300 seeds (measured here; Q4).
- **The mesh is not.** Delaunator uses only exactly specified operations apart from one `Math.pow(2, -52)` constant. Round 4's three determinism fixes should make regeneration engine-independent, "though no browser has tested it". — [round 4 world-maps.md, Q1](../../round-4-multi-scale/notes/world-maps.md); [round 4 report](../../round-4-multi-scale/report.md) (opened).

### Inferences

**Why the grid wins**
- **One source of truth.** The mesh's only real advantage is organic coasts and rivers. For a GBA-style map that advantage disappears once the mesh is rasterised to 8- and 16-px tiles, which round 4's own "Option B" proposed. A mesh would then be a second representation that the renderer must re-derive. A settlement on a mesh cell could even rasterise onto water.
- **Exact cells.** Every cell covers the same area, so land, farmland, forest and catchments are integer cell counts, with no float areas to apportion. Regions and market territories become one Uint16 label per cell from a multi-source Dijkstra on the same grid.
- **Simpler proof.** Grid neighbour order is fixed and integer-only, and it has been proven equal across engines. A mesh adds float point coordinates and a third-party triangulator to the determinism surface.

**Settlement counts**
- **Keep 96×64 as the standard world.** It fits the Country view at 8 px (768×512 px) and matches the reference and its art.
- **Offer a "large" 192×128 world (182–237 listed places).** Its JS generation should take about 50–150 ms, scaling the estimate by its 4× cells (inference).
- **Treat 384×256 (938 places) as a stress test, not a default.** The Country view would no longer fit a screen at 8 px, and the rank rule would need retuning, since only hamlets grow.
- **Listed settlements get ledgers.** The unlisted ranks between one listed place's true rank and the next fold into the region tier. M7 already plans that tier "for unlisted hamlets". This split is exact, because the ranks are known integers.
- **Smaller ledger load.** The standard world runs about 50 settlement ledgers and 50 region accounts. At round 4's 0.5 µs each, that is near 0.05 ms per day (computed). The 1,000- and 10,000-settlement budgets remain as stress tiers.
- **Zipf on true ranks.** M7's ζ test (0.9–1.2) must fit true ranks or all ranks. Fitting on listed rank k would wrongly read the deliberate steeper display rule as a failed Zipf law.

**Plan changes**
- **M8 terrain task.** Drop "jittered-grid or shipped points, Delaunator, template plus noise elevation …". Instead, port tools/worldgen's stages to TS on a square grid: terrain, drainage, climate and biomes. Prove each against per-stage Python goldens.
- **M8 settlements and routes.** These stay algorithmically the same (habitability, spacing, rank-size; MST plus a 1.5× spanner plus A* with a road-reuse discount) but run on the grid. Add sea lanes between landmasses through coastal or port cells.
- **M8 drawing.** Draw Country and Region as 8- and 16-px tilemaps of the same cells. Drop the "small palette-quantised framebuffer" option and the re-meshed Region view.
- **M8 exit check.** Drop "1,000 settlements on 30,000 cells in ≤ 1.5 s". In desktop Chromium, a standard 96×64 world should generate in ≤ 100 ms, and a large 192×128 world in ≤ 400 ms. ### Takeaway

**Semantics behind the traps.**
- Python floor division "is that of mathematical division with the 'floor' function applied to the result". Modulo "always yields a result with the same sign as its second operand", with `x == (x//y)*y + (x%y)` and `divmod(x, y) == (x//y, x%y)`. "A right shift by n bits is defined as floor division by pow(2,n)." — [Python reference, expressions](https://docs.python.org/3/reference/expressions.html) (opened).
- JavaScript `%` "always takes the sign of the dividend". MDN gives `((n % d) + d) % d` for a true modulo. — [MDN Remainder](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Operators/Remainder) (opened).
- `>>>` is zero-filling, "so the resulting number is always positive", while `>>` propagates the sign: `-9 >> 2` is −3 but `-9 >>> 2` is 1073741821. — [MDN Unsigned right shift](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Operators/Unsigned_right_shift) (opened).
- `Math.sqrt` is not on ECMAScript's list of "implementation-approximated" Math functions — round 4, [world-maps.md Q1](../../round-4-multi-scale/notes/world-maps.md) (opened there).

**Floor and square root in doubles** (computed). For integers with |a| < 2^53, `Math.floor(a / b)` equals Python's `a // b`. For a non-integer quotient, the gap to the next integer is at least 1/|b|. The rounding error is at most |q|·2^−53, so crossing the gap needs |a| ≥ 2^53. The same argument makes `Math.floor(Math.sqrt(n))` exact for n < 2^52. The prototype's `isqrt` still corrects ±1, so it never depends on that bound. The largest intermediate in `terrain._near_chain` (cross², up to about 2 × 10^11) is far below 2^53 but above 2^31. It must never pass through `| 0`, `Int32Array` or `Math.imul`.

### Inferences

**How to port, in order**
1. **Integer helpers first.** Write `floorDiv`, `mod`, `isqrt` and a debug-only `assertSafe(|x| < 2^53)`. Lint-ban bare `/` and `%` in `worldgen` code outside those helpers, plus the existing `Math.random`, transcendental `Math` and `**` bans. Draw results stay `uint32` (`>>> 0`), and lattice reads use `>>>`.
2. **Then stages in pipeline order.** Port `rng`, `noise`, `grid`, `terrain`, `climate.rain`, `drainage`, the rest of `climate`, `settle`, `roads`, `features` and `world`, then `place` and `looks`. Commit each stage only when its golden fingerprints match. `place.py` (1,737 lines) is the largest and should come last.
3. **Heaps and sorts carry no hidden order.** `drainage.flood` pops `(level, draw, cell)`, `roads._walk` pops `(f, g, cell)`, and `place.route` pops `(cost, seq, …)`. Each key is a total order, so any correct binary heap pops the same sequence. Every Python `sorted` key seen ends in an index or cell id, so JS comparators must too. String keys need plain `<` comparisons, never `localeCompare`.
4. **Dict order needs explicit arrays.** `features.landmarks` iterates `CHANCES` and `place.decorate` sorts `site.places.items()`. Python dicts keep insertion order, as do JS `Map`s and objects with non-numeric keys. Use arrays anyway, so the order is stated rather than inherited.
5. **Pick types by range.** `Int32Array` suits cell columns. Products such as cross², (x − cx)²·ONE and people × people may exceed 2^31, so they stay in plain doubles until divided.
6. **Fix order-dependent passes before freezing goldens.** `place.py`'s `tidy_water` floods in raster order, which a port must copy exactly. Switching the reference to plan-then-apply first, as the editor research recommends, makes every pass order-free. It also means the first goldens never pin the raster behaviour.

**How to prove equivalence**
- **Goldens.** `stages.py` is the template. It writes 15 eight-hex-digit hashes per seed, 363 B of compact JSON (measured here). For 300 seeds rotating through 3 grid sizes, as `golden_shape.py` does, that is about 110 KB (computed). That file becomes a committed test fixture per generator version.
- **The CI job** runs the TS port against it in Node, Bun (JavaScriptCore, the Safari proxy), and Playwright Chromium, Firefox and WebKit. The first failing stage names the module to fix.
- **Kernels** get the `vectors.py` differential fuzzer, which costs 0.3–0.4 s per run.
- **After the switch.** Once the TS generator is canonical, freeze the Python as that version's oracle or retire it. The goldens remain the contract.
- **Speed.** Python's 1.66 s per country falls to an estimated 15–35 ms in JS (inference). The terrain-stage ratio of 90–100× gives 17–18 ms, and the margin allows for heap-heavy stages porting less well. That is far inside M8's 1.5 s bar, so generation can run at "new game" without a progress bar.

### Gaps

- Bun and Deno are not installed here, and Playwright had no WebKit or Firefox build. JavaScriptCore and SpiderMonkey are therefore unmeasured. The kernels use only spec-exact integer operations, so a match is expected, but that is an inference.
- Only `rng`, `noise`, `grid` and the terrain stage were ported here. The editor research added `drainage.flood` and the shore rule. The other later stages and most of `place.py` were not ported. Their heaps, sorts and dict-order risks are reasoned from the code, not run.
- All timings come from one busy Windows desktop (20–60% CPU from other processes, low free RAM). None is a phone timing.

- **Totals.** About 26 tiles unblock Country and Region, or 50 with cliff coasts. About 29–31 unblock streets, wonders and routes. That is 55–81 tiles, against 169 existing map, scenery and nature frames: 45 + 72 + 52 (computed).
- **Snow comes before its tiles.** It needs a generator rule first: today no cell can be "snow", so tiles alone would never be drawn.
- **Never add these.** Shabby or run-down building variants stay banned by content rule 5, including round 4's district-wealth prefabs. Snow, rock and sand are terrain only. They never mark a culture or a group, and culture shows through decorations, per the sprites README.
- **If walk-in interiors are wanted later:**

  - about 10 props for each of about 10 building kinds (home forms, shop, clinic, school, town hall, police station, records office, courthouse, jail, workplace);
  
  that is about 110 sprites, used as one hand-made interior per kind with keyed dressing (Q1).

### Gaps

- **Unseen overlays.** The map-scale overlays were not drawn, so whether 12 cases read well at 8 px is untested. Round 4's region mockup (`docs/mockups/country_map.png`, opened) is the target look: light coast edges, with rivers and routes as lines.
- **Unvalidated snow threshold.** The cut near temperature 40 is a placeholder; it needs a look at cold worlds.
- **Untried line style.** Lines-versus-tiles for rivers and roads at 16 px is an art-direction call; no players were asked.
- **Unchecked colours.** The new tiles' palette fit and outline contrast (the plan's 3:1 bar against walkable tiles) were not checked.
