# Learned customs, culture-blind judgement

Round 8 report: cultures. Written 6 October 2026 from the three notes in [`notes/`](notes/) and the questions in [`questions.md`](questions.md). Every figure keeps the evidence label its note gave it.

## Bottom line

The owner asked for blobs with traits of their own, and chose learned cultures: fictional cultures with harmless customs (favourite foods, festivals, music, naming and a home region) that children learn and adults can mix or switch. Nomos can have them at almost no cost, if one rule holds. **Culture shapes demand and leisure, never any judgement, and it never appears where crime is shown.** It may change what people buy above subsistence, when and where they gather, what music they seek, how their names are structured and which region they call home. It never enters offending, victim choice, patrols, stops, arrests, reporting, hiring, wages, productivity, saving, credit or inheritance.

- **People: 6 bytes each.**
  - A current culture, a birth culture, four customs each stored as the culture it came from, and a birth-region id.
  - At most 8 cultures per world, matching round 6's eight-class map palette.
  - Memory rises from about 170 to about 176 B of the 256 B cap (computed).
- **Transmission: customs fade over three generations, and structure keeps a town mixed.**
  - In the US in 2000, 85% of second-generation Hispanic children spoke at least some Spanish at home, against 28% in the third generation (Alba, opened; the 28% computed from 72% speaking only English).
  - Intermarriage is the main brake, and it rises by generation.
  - Imitation alone collapses diversity. Minority parents trying harder, partners from their own culture, newcomers and conformist learning sustain it (opened and measured here).
  - A closed town with conformist learning lost its small cultures within 150–200 years. An inflow of 0.5% a year held the largest culture's share near 0.55 over a century (measured here).
  - Adults update every 30 days, about 77 µs a day in reference-machine terms at 100k agents (RM-eq, computed).
- **Customs: preferences only, sized like real differences, equal in cost and time.**
  - Food: culture shifts only the discretionary Stone–Geary shares, by at most ±25% of the neutral share. That is about how much countries at similar incomes differ (computed from ICP 2021).
  - Subsistence, total spending, saving, work and grade never read culture. So culture should not cause hunger, which is what drives food theft; an M2 exit check tests it.
  - Festivals: every culture gets the same festival time, 8–12 days a year, in leisure time only, so culture never closes a workplace. Attending counts as social contact.
  - Names: generated by the UI and never read by the sim; naming customs set the name's structure.
  - Home regions grow from 4–8 hearths on the country map, and migration carries culture exactly.
- **Respect: structure, not labels.**
  - Every game the ethics note reviewed that tied culture or species to ability, honesty, wealth or violence produced group stereotypes. That includes Norland, which calls its cultures learned and in November 2025 gave one culture's migrants "twice as much money" (opened).
  - Abstract labels alone do not prevent stereotypes. People link a smaller group with rare bad acts (r = 0.34 over 28 tests), more strongly for negative acts and longer streams of events (Mullen and Johnson, opened). A crime simulation is such a stream.
  - So culture never appears on any crime surface, the culture lens is opt-in and hides justice cues, wording avoids generic claims about a culture, and a diverse panel checks that no culture reads as a real people.
- **Guardrails: four test layers, because culture-blind rules are not enough.**
  - A package boundary with lint, a relabel test, a threshold-level flip test and an outcome and exposure audit.
  - In toy tests whose rules never read culture, clustered housing alone gave 3.2× stop and 8.2× arrest gaps between cultures. Evening festivals raised one culture's victimisation by 8.7% through exposure, not treatment (measured here).
  - So housing ignores culture, customs cost the same and keep the same hours, and patrols never read culture or festivals.
- **Budgets: yes, with the country tier in the planned WASM port.**
  - Agents add 6 B and about 0.08 ms RM-eq a day at 100k (computed).
  - Settlements add 66 B, still under the 1 KB cap (computed).
  - At 10,000 settlements, culture adds up to about 1.3 ms RM-eq a day, and the save grows by 50–118 KB gzip (computed and measured here).

On 6 October 2026 the owner accepted all seven recommended defaults:
- the inspector and lens show an inherited culture label, while practice drifts underneath;
- City mode brings in culturally different newcomers at 0.5% a year;
- housing allows no residential clustering by culture;
- partners pair by shared customs;
- names use one shared sound set;
- every culture gets the same daytime and evening festival mix;
- the content rule's name filter uses edit distance 1 for names up to 5 letters.

Every timing in this round comes from one desktop running Node; none is a phone timing.

## 1. Transmission and mixing

### What the evidence says

- **Each custom passes on at its own strength.** Cavalli-Sforza's index of vertical transmission averages 0.77 for religion and 0.61 for politics, against 0.19 for entertainment and 0.09 for habits ([Science 1982](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7123211), opened via a scanned copy).
- **Heritage practice fades over about three generations.**
  - US census 2000, children aged 6–15: 85% of second-generation Hispanic children spoke at least some Spanish at home, and 61% of Asian children an Asian language. In the third generation, 72% and 92% spoke only English ([CCIS working paper 111](https://ccis.ucsd.edu/_files/wp111.pdf), opened).
  - Canada: immigrant mothers passed their mother tongue to 41% of children in 1981. Their daughters passed it to 23% in 2006, so about 10% of grandchildren kept it ([Houle 2011](https://web.archive.org/web/2023/https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-008-x/2011002/article/11453-eng.htm), opened via the Wayback Machine).
- **Intermarriage is the main brake, and it rises by generation.**
  - US newlyweds: 15% of immigrant and 39% of US-born Hispanics married out, and 24% and 46% of Asians ([Pew 2017](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2017/05/intermarriage-may-2017-full-report.pdf), opened).
  - In Canada, union type "alone explains almost all" of the drop in transmission (Houle, opened).
  - Indian migrant couples where both spouses moved keep origin food tastes 5.3× as strongly as couples where one did (computed from [Atkin, NBER w19196](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w19196/w19196.pdf), opened).
- **Customs blend over decades.** About 60% of a mover's brand-share gap closes at once, and half the rest takes more than 20 years ([Bronnenberg, Dubé and Gentzkow](https://web.stanford.edu/~gentzkow/research/brands.pdf), opened). Immigrant parents closed half the naming gap in 20 years in 1920, and a third in California today ([NBER w22381](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22381/w22381.pdf), opened). That is a yearly adoption hazard of 2.0–3.4% (computed).
- **Models: imitation alone collapses diversity.**
  - Bisin–Verdier socialisation homogenises when parental effort is fixed, and keeps a stable mix when minority parents try harder ([Annual Review of Economics 2023](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/c/16384/files/2024/01/bisin-verdier-2023-advances-in-the-economic-theory-of-cultural-transmission.pdf), opened). Continuous blending of traits always homogenises (opened).
  - Axelrod's model keeps about 2 regions at 100 × 100 sites ([J. Conflict Resolution 1997](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022002797041002001), opened). A mutation rate of 10⁻³ leaves 1.55 regions, and one ordered feature gives one culture in 100 of 100 runs ([Flache and Macy](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0604201), opened).
  - Conformity is real but uneven: 28 of 40 lab learners conformed, with strength D ≈ 0.38 ([Efferson et al. 2008](http://www.des.ucdavis.edu/faculty/richerson/EffsConfMavericks.pdf), opened).
- **Schelling sorting is easy to trigger.** Demanding that half of one's neighbours be alike gives over 4 : 1 like-to-unlike neighbours, and a 10% minority forms clusters of 100 or more ([Schelling 1971](https://www.suz.uzh.ch/dam/jcr:00000000-68cb-72db-ffff-ffffff8071db/04.02_schelling_71.pdf), opened). Real US immigrant dissimilarity averaged 0.35–0.56 from 1910 to 2000 ([Cutler, Glaeser and Vigdor](https://content.csbs.utah.edu/~philips/soccer2/readings_files/Vigdor%20Is%20the%20Melting%20Pot%20Still%20Hot%20Explaining%20the%20Resurgence%20of%20Immigrant%20Segregation.pdf), opened).

### What the prototype found

The town model ([town.mjs](prototypes/transmission/town.mjs)) ran 100 simulated years with 8 cultures, 16 districts and 0.5% a year culturally different newcomers. It started from one large culture (60%) and seven smaller ones, to compare against migrant data. The country model ([ledger.mjs](prototypes/transmission/ledger.mjs)) ran 1,000 settlements. All figures are measured here.

- **The baseline town stays diverse and mixes realistically** (3 seeds each at 10k and 100k).
  - The largest culture's share went from 0.59–0.61 to 0.553–0.560, and the effective number of cultures from 2.47–2.59 to 2.86–2.92.
  - Second-generation children kept 48% of the heritage food custom and third-generation children 13–14%.
  - Exogamy rose from 27–34% in the first generation to 35–41% in the second.
- **A closed town loses its minorities.** With conformist learning and no newcomers, minority identities vanished within 150–200 years, with or without stronger minority parenting.
- **Inflow sets the balance.** Culturally different arrivals of 0.25%, 0.5% and 1% a year left the initially largest culture at 0.79, 0.55 and 0.21 of the town after 100 years.
- **The country keeps regions, and capitals become melting pots.** With 20% of movers going anywhere, regional distinctiveness (G_ST) fell from 0.733 to 0.239 without acculturation and to 0.544 with conformity. Capitals reached 2.0–4.2 effective cultures, while villages stayed at 1.1–1.6. 94.8–95.7% of settlements kept their dominant culture.
- **Checking every 30 days gives the same outcomes as daily checks** at about 1/23 of the cost.
- **Determinism holds.** The same seed gave the same hash, and reversing the visiting order gave identical hashes.

### Design

- **Customs are nominal, never a blend.** Each custom records which culture's version a person holds. Linear mixing drives everyone to one culture (inference from opened sources).
- **At birth**, a custom both parents share is kept with a fidelity that rises from 0.5 to f₁ as it becomes locally rare: 0.9 for food and naming, 0.95 for festivals, 0.5 for music. When parents differ, vertical transmission succeeds with probability 0.6 and follows one lead parent 75% of the time. Otherwise the child learns from five district adults, with conformity 0.3 (design values tested in the prototype).
- **Adults** adopt customs on a 30-day stride from per-district counts. Yearly copying rates are 3% for food, 1% for festivals, 2.5% for naming and 1% for music, or 15% for music at ages 10–24. Rates are halved for people holding all four of their own customs, raised 1.5× for two and doubled for one or none.
- **Switching is gradual, caused by contact and symmetric.** No culture is a default or a target. The inspector and lens show the inherited label, as the owner chose; the current culture still tracks practice, switching at 25% a year once three of the four customs come from one other culture. The person keeps their name, and the inspector shows their custom history.
- **Partners** in the prototype prefer their own culture: 0.2 plus 0.1 for each own custom kept, applied by both partners, never reading hue. The recommended form weights candidates by shared customs instead (see (l)).
- **Diversity comes from connections.** A closed town will realistically lose small minorities in three to five generations. "No collapse" is a property of the connected country and of City mode's boundary inflow (inference).
- **Housing never reads culture.** Neighbour-culture preferences are the "xenophobia mechanics" that round 6 listed under "Avoid". Mild clustering could come only from kin placement, as a labelled knob that is off by default (inference).

**Gaps.** No migrant study measures festival or music transmission, so those rates are analogies. The model makes too few coherent teenagers of migrant descent: 4.5–5.3% heritage-only and 7–8% host-only, against 22.5% and 18.7% in Berry et al.'s 13-country study (opened). First-generation exogamy (27–34%) sits above the US band (15–24%), partly because Nomos minorities are small. Alba et al. 2002, Rumbaut et al. 2006 and Bisin, Topa and Verdier 2004 were seen only as search summaries.

## 2. Customs in the simulation

### Food and goods

- **Income explains little of what countries eat within the food budget.** Across 173 countries in ICP 2021, income explains 1–37% of the variation in each category's share of food spending. After income and own price, the residual SD is 4.4–8.4 points, 25–38% of the mean share (computed with [icp_taste.mjs](prototypes/customs/icp_taste.mjs) from the World Bank ICP API, opened).
- **Tastes travel with people.** Indian interstate migrants keep their origin state's food shares and forgo 1.6% of calories on average, up to 7.0% where origin foods are dear (Atkin, opened). US movers' past brand experience depreciates at about 2.6% a year (Bronnenberg, Dubé and Gentzkow, opened).
- **Design: culture moves only the marginal shares.**
  - In the Stone–Geary rule, culture changes only β, the shares of spending above subsistence, across the six food categories plus one wares-against-services split.
  - Shifts sum to zero, stay within ±25% of the neutral share (about 0.7–1.0 residual SD), and keep every category at 0.6× its neutral share or more. ±40% is for lab cards only.
  - Subsistence γ is in portions, bought cheapest-first, so unmet food need should not depend on culture (checked in M2). Unmet need drives food theft, the food-insecurity tally and the wellbeing food penalty, so this one rule keeps culture out of crime and welfare.
  - Total spending, saving, labour supply, the food total share and grade never read culture.
- **Favourite foods come from place, not from themes.** Until M8 adds hearths, a keyed draw assigns each culture's favoured categories. From M8 they follow what the hearth region produces in abundance, like Victoria 3's "obsessions" that "can emerge organically" ([Dev Diary #56](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/529340/view/4514316500003104117), opened, Steam News API). That ties tastes to harvests and lowers the chance of a real people's food stereotype (inference).
- **Welfare stays put by construction.** At the default band and ±10% local price gaps, a culture's basket costs at most 2.5% more or less of food spending: 0.2–1.1% of consumption across the four presets, or 1–5 milli-ladder counted as lost income (computed with [welfare_bound.mjs](prototypes/customs/welfare_bound.mjs)).
- **Prices move, honestly.** A culture's home region will show local premia on its favourite categories, largest for fresh food. A per-settlement "basket cost by culture" line should stay within about ±2.5% of food spending. It is a diagnostic in headless reports, not a correction (inference).
- **A household's shift** is the mean of its adults' shifts, recomputed only when the household or a culture changes.

### Festivals and music

- **Frequency.** Countries keep a median of 12 nationwide public holidays a year (p10–p90 7–16, range 3–25, 204 countries; computed with [holiday_counts.mjs](prototypes/customs/holiday_counts.mjs) from the Nager.Date API, opened).
- **Pull.** 32% of US adults attended a performance in a park or outdoor space in 2017, and 62% of performing-arts participants say they go to spend time with family and friends ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/news/press-releases/2020/national-endowment-arts-releases-latest-survey-public-participation-arts), opened). Socialising doubles on rest days and holidays, from 0.44 to 0.92 hours a day ([ATUS 2025](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/atus.t02.htm), fetch-tool summary).
- **Spending spikes are sharp for one food and small for the month.** US grocery sales run only 4.5% above the year's daily rate in December, against 31% for liquor stores (computed with [grocery_seasonality.py](prototypes/customs/grocery_seasonality.py) from Census retail data, opened).
- **Design.**
  - Each culture gets 4–6 festivals totalling 8–12 days a year, one of them a 2–3-day major festival, spread across the year and open to everyone. Every culture gets the same total, which keeps expected contact and wellbeing equal.
  - Festivals fall only in leisure time, on rest days or evenings, so culture never closes a workplace or changes hours worked. By default every culture gets the same mix of daytime and evening festival days (see (n)).
  - Attending counts as social contact and resets the isolation counter. People who mix cultures choose among more festivals but never get more festival days. There is no separate wellbeing bonus by default.
  - Demand for the festival's favoured foods rises 2–4× on the day (unsourced estimate), paid from one equal per-person festival budget within the month's discretionary spending, never from subsistence. Shops stock ahead from the public calendar, so prices barely spike and leftover fresh food spoils.
  - Music is an abstract preference (tempo, loudness, structure) with invented style names, never a real genre. Music events at the square or park buy services; any services worker may perform any style.
- **Left out on purpose.** In Udaipur, the median extremely poor household spends 10% of its annual budget on festivals ([Banerjee and Duflo](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/The%20Economic%20Lives%20of%20the%20Poor.pdf), opened). Nomos never lets festival or taste spending crowd out food, and the "What this toy leaves out" page should say so.

### Names

- **Generation is cheap and deterministic:** about 220 names per ms, with 85–98% unique given names in 5,000 draws per culture (measured here with [names.mjs](prototypes/customs/names.mjs)). Names carry no gender, because Nomos bodies have none.
- **Invented sound sets still evoke real languages.**
  - A trigram screen recovered all 33 real-language control sets; franc language ID recovered only 12. Open-syllable designs leaned Finnic, Hawaiian, Castilian or West African. Three designs with moderately complex syllables scored 0.21–0.23 against a review bar of 0.26 and passed (measured here with [names_screen.mjs](prototypes/customs/names_screen.mjs)).
  - A naming-language designer found "Hawaiian sounds" feel different from "Arabic ones". He dropped one language per map region because "it was too hard to make it clear that this was what was going on" ([O'Leary](https://web.archive.org/web/2020/http://mewo2.com/notes/naming-language/), opened via the Wayback Machine).
- **Names cue group membership.** Résumés with White-sounding names got 50% more callbacks than identical ones with African-American-sounding names ([Bertrand and Mullainathan](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w9873/w9873.pdf), opened).
- **Design (adopted in (k)).** All personal names come from one shared invented sound set, without diacritics, screened once against real name bases. Naming customs set only the structure: given and family name, given and parent's given name, or given name "of" home place. Given names are drawn fresh, as in Dwarf Fortress, where "neither name is inherited from the parents" (opened, community wiki). Where a custom uses family names, they pass on by that custom's rule, such as from the lead parent. The UI builds names from (seed, id, birth culture), the sim never reads them, and site words stay separate from stems, as in "Kelvi Ford", so names do not read English.
- **The planned "edit distance ≤ 2" filter is too blunt for short names.** It rejected about 42% of 3- and 4-letter names against Pokémon place names, and 18–35% of all names with the other rules. Matching only distinctive town and city names, at distance 1 up to 5 letters and 2 above, cut total rejections to 2.0–4.6% (measured here). A second fixture should reject names near real countries, demonyms, languages, ethnonyms and religions (inference).

### Home regions and migration

- **Regions:** place 4–8 hearths by keyed Poisson-disc, grow regions by multi-source Dijkstra on M8's travel-cost mesh, and mix a border band. Fantasy Map Generator also grows cultures by multi-source Dijkstra, but with `Math.random` and one culture per cell ([cultures-generator.ts](https://github.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/blob/b944003c14/src/generators/cultures-generator.ts), MIT, opened).
- **Regions never line up with poverty.** Each culture's territory should span settlements of different development, and most development regions should hold two or more cultures; world generation flags any that hold one (inference from the ethics note).
- **A person's home region** is their birth settlement's region, and it never changes.
- **Migration never reads culture, and splits every flow by the origin's culture counts with keyed stochastic rounding.** Plain largest remainder is exact too, but it hands every leftover person to the biggest culture. Under largest remainder, a settlement with a 2% minority and flows of 1–3 people a month sent no minority members in 30 years, against 14.4 expected; keyed stochastic rounding sent 21 (measured here).
- **Spin-up:** run the country ledger 50–100 years before play, so cities become melting pots first. A 100-year spin-up costs about 0.14 s for 1,000 settlements on the desktop (computed).

## 3. Prior art and respect

### What games did

- **The major games reviewed tie culture or species to mechanics Nomos forbids.**
  - Crusader Kings III's Bellicose ethos gives "+2 Prowess" and "+10% Levy Size" ([CK3 Wiki](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Culture), opened, WebFetch extract).
  - Stellaris traits are a species' "innate functions, abilities, and personality" ([Species traits](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Species_traits)); Intelligent gives "+10% Researcher job efficiency" ([Biological traits](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Biological_traits); both Stellaris Wiki, opened, WebFetch extract).
  - RimWorld's Aggressive gene doubles social fights and Hyper-aggressive triples them; Hussars carry the latter ([RimWorld Wiki: Genes, rev 184104](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Genes), opened, community wiki).
  - Dwarf Fortress goblins treat theft, lying and assault as a "Personal matter" ([DF Wiki: Ethic, rev 320629](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Ethic), opened, community wiki).
- **Calling culture "learned" is not enough.** Norland calls culture "a social construct rather than a genetic one", yet gave cultures work skills, xenophobic riots and repression ([Devlog #12](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1857090/view/5322753221339713464), opened, Steam News API). It removed cultural work bonuses in May 2025 ([Patch #37](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1857090/view/1799817379503234)), then gave Makha migrants "twice as much money" in November 2025 ([Patch #43, beta](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1857090/view/1816849002015273); both opened, Steam News API).
- **Victoria 3 puts discrimination in law, not culture.** Acceptance from 0 to 100 comes mainly from Citizenship and Church & State laws, and its United States starts under Racial Segregation ([Dev Diaries #47](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/529340/view/4292505348825755143) and [#129](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/529340/view/6339469370182122616), opened, Steam News API). That is correct about institutions, but in a crime toy it would put real racial disparities on screen under fictional labels. Nomos names it on the "What this toy leaves out" page instead (inference).
- **Players reject forced identity change.** Civilization VII's update of 19 May 2026 lets players "start and stay as any civilization", answering complaints about lost agency and jarring forced transitions ([Time-Tested Civs](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-guide/gameplay/time-tested-civs/), opened, WebFetch extract; date from a search summary). Humankind's culture switching drew similar complaints (search summary).
- **Real cultures need consultation.** The Poundmaker Cree Nation objected in 2018 that nobody consulted it about its portrayal in Civilization VI ([CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.4473089), opened).
- **Prestige hierarchies leak.** CK3's court languages form a "pecking order" ([Dev Diary #78](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1158310/view/4698935641773317560), opened, Steam News API). RimWorld has "neolithic" and "savage" tribes ([Tribes, rev 183576](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Tribes), opened, community wiki). Nomos ranks no culture, language or stage of "development".

### Shapes worth borrowing without the payloads

- **CK3's acceptance**, a symmetric 0–100% number per pair of cultures raised by contact ([Dev Diary #64](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1158310/view/5019805792179151642), opened, Steam News API), becomes familiarity that speeds learning only, never an opinion penalty.
- **RimWorld's certainty** falls 6% per conversion attempt before modifiers and recovers 1–3% a day ([Certainty, rev 183826](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Certainty)). A child adopts a belief at age 3, weighted by who interacted with it ([Children](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Children); both opened, community wiki). These match the transmission design's gradual, contact-driven learning.
- **Victoria 3's obsessions** emerging from local abundance become favourite foods from place (section 2).
- **Dwarf Fortress** generates a language per world ([Divine language](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Divine_language)), draws names fresh ([Name](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Name)) and generates musical forms "by culture" ([Performer](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Performer); all DF Wiki, opened, community wiki). These become the shared sound set and abstract music styles.

### What research says about stereotypes

- **Abstract labels do not stop stereotypes forming.** In Hamilton and Gifford's design, observers linked a smaller "Group B" with rare bad acts (search summary). Mullen and Johnson's meta-analysis found r = 0.344 over 28 frequency-estimate tests and r = 0.259 over 23 group-assignment tests. The effect was stronger for negative behaviour and grew with the number of examples ([Brit. J. Soc. Psychol. 1990](https://bulidomics.com/w/images/2/29/Mullen1990.pdf), opened, third-party PDF copy).
- **Wording can essentialise a fictional group.** Generic sentences about the invented "Zarpies" raised adults' odds of essentialist answers 6.07× [4.56–8.08] against unlabelled sentences ([Rhodes et al., PNAS 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3427061/), opened).
- **Lessons about fictional groups transfer to real ones.** In a trial with 34 children (17 per arm), six weekly Harry Potter sessions improved attitudes toward immigrants, only for those who identified with Harry ([Vezzali et al. 2015](https://sparq.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj19021/files/media/file/vezzali_et_al._2015_-_the_greatest_magic_of_harry_potter.pdf), opened; weak evidence).
- **Classroom simulations of fictional cultures can backfire.** BaFá BaFá raised students' ethnocentrism and dogmatism (search summary). In Tiltfactor's small studies, a deck with fewer bias-themed cards worked better: 89% against 63% other-oriented choices ([Kaufman and Flanagan 2015](https://cyberpsychology.eu/article/view/4343), opened, WebFetch extract).
- **Strength of evidence.** Illusory correlation is meta-analytic and robust in the lab. The generic-language results come from well-controlled experiments by one lab. Transfer and game studies are small. No study was found that tests whether an abstract group in a simulation changes stereotypes of real groups.

### Keeping fictional cultures from reading as real

- **Fictional peoples read as real when they borrow a real people's markers.** Wizards of the Coast said in 2020 that orcs and drow had been described in ways "painfully reminiscent" of how real groups are denigrated, and made ability increases changeable ([2020 statement](https://web.archive.org/web/2020/https://dnd.wizards.com/articles/features/diversity-and-dnd), opened via the Wayback Machine). In 2022 it replaced "race" with "species" in coordination with outside cultural consultants ([D&D Beyond](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1393-moving-on-from-race-in-one-d-d), opened, WebFetch extract). Monson describes World of Warcraft's trolls speaking with "a stereotypical Jamaican accent" ([Games and Culture 2012](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=1023f3922c7417dcc752eb8da9557520b07a3a52&repid=rep1&type=pdf), opened).
- **The markers are dress, accent, music genre, religion, architecture, food themes and naming sounds.** Rule 8 already removes dress and bodies. Names, music, festivals and food are the open channels, closed by the shared sound set, abstract music styles, open festivals and favourite foods from place (inference).
- **No hierarchy words.** Avoid "primitive", "savage", "tribe", "barbarian", "neolithic", "civilised", "advanced", "native", "foreign", "exotic" and "race" (inference).
- **Customs are a toy layer.** Educators warn against the "tourist" curriculum of foods and holidays (Derman-Sparks, search summary). The "What this toy leaves out" page should say real cultures are far richer.
- **Real customs have been policed through music and gathering rules.** London's mayor ordered a review of Form 696, saying it should not "unfairly target one community or music genre" ([Mayor of London, 2017](https://www.london.gov.uk/press-releases/mayoral/mayor-orders-review-of-form-696), opened, WebFetch extract). New York's 1926 cabaret law was used against Harlem jazz clubs until its 2017 repeal (search summary). Nomos therefore has no offence or report type tied to customs.

### Wording and the culture lens

- **Wording.** Say "people raised with Velan customs" or "Velan festival", never "the Velans" as a kind of people. Use shares and ranges ("6 in 10 pick fish"), never generics ("Velans love fish"). Describe change as learning, and compare cultures only on preferences (inference).
- **The opt-in culture lens.**
  - Off by default, like the wealth lens, and never used in preview cards, share clips or default replays.
  - It shows customs only: district shares of each custom as small multiples, the festival calendar, the home-regions map mode in country view, and a person's custom mix and history in a Customs tab of the inspector.
  - While it is on, crime and justice cues are hidden: act rings, justice bubbles, record markers, patrol overlays and the true and recorded panels. Turning on a justice view turns the lens off. Records and customs sit in separate inspector tabs that never show together.
  - Its palette avoids the six body hues and the reds and oranges round 3 keeps for crime, and adds icons or patterns so colour is never the only cue (WCAG 1.4.1). Culture emblems drafted on the unmerged `feat/pixel-sprites` branch aim to follow this; no note checked them (unverified).

## 4. Guardrails

### Five channels that could leak culture into crime or wealth

| Channel | How culture could reach crime or wealth | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Inheritance drift | Culture is learned at home, wealth passes to heirs, and households sort by fear and price, so culture could track district, patrols and records (round 3's warning) | Spawn culture on its own keyed stream, independent of wealth rank, home and job. Housing and Schelling moves never read culture; partner choice follows (l). Audit culture against wealth decile and district |
| Custom costs | A pricier favourite food or festival lowers savings, raises unmet need and so raises food theft | Customs swap goods within the budget and never add spending; one equal per-person festival budget; subsistence bought cheapest-first |
| Time and place | Night festivals put one culture in front of patrols, witnesses and thieves more often | Equal festival days, an equal daytime and evening mix by default, festivals open to all. Patrols never read culture, festivals or crowds. No offence or report type tied to customs |
| Home regions | In country mode a culture's region could sit in a poor development preset | Each culture spans settlements of different development; world generation flags development regions that hold one culture; migration never reads culture |
| Visible cues | Culture-styled names, a culture tint or an inspector column on crime events builds an illusory correlation | One shared sound set; justice views show case numbers and roles; the culture lens hides justice cues |

The table merges the ethics note's channels with the toy measurements below (inference).

### Four test layers

The transmission and customs notes independently reached the same four layers, from cheap to strong; the ethics note adds the outcome bands.

1. **A static boundary.** Culture code lives in its own package, `sim-culture`. A dependency-cruiser `reachable` rule bars crime, policing, labour, wage, wealth, ability, housing and migration code from reaching it, even through helpers. An ESLint profile bans its import and its column names, whether read by property, destructuring or brackets. Splits by culture, such as dividing a migration flow or apportioning spawned households, live inside `sim-culture` and act on totals that culture-blind code produced. In a scratch project, ESLint caught all four direct violations but missed a transitive import, which dependency-cruiser caught (measured here).
2. **A relabel test.** Permute culture ids together with their custom rows; every non-culture state hash must stay identical. Clean code passed on 10 of 10 seeds in one prototype and 3 of 3 in the other, and every planted id-reading leak failed (measured here). The test is blind to code that reads customs, by design. Because culture-level draws are keyed by a stable uid, it would also miss a guarded draw keyed the same way; the flip test covers that.
3. **A flip test on thresholds and draw keys.** Shuffle the culture column, hold behaviour fixed, and recompute every guarded decision's integer threshold and draw key before its draw.
   - Clean code changed 0 of 3.69 million thresholds. Three planted leaks changed 4,136, 67,044 and 35,417 thresholds, but only 4, 93 and 0 realised stops (measured here).
   - In the other prototype, an id leak changed 3 of 40,000 decisions but 531 of 20,000 thresholds. A draw keyed on culture changed 231 decisions and no thresholds (measured here).
   - So the test compares thresholds and draw keys, not only yes/no outcomes. Every guarded decision must be written as an integer threshold followed by one keyed draw whose key never includes culture.
4. **An outcome and exposure audit** over 50 paired seeds, in headless CI.
   - Raw outcomes: each culture's rates of true offending, victimisation, stops, wrongful stops, arrests and records, and its mean wealth decile, sit within 0.9–1.1 of the population rate (a proposed band for the 90% interval of the ratio).
   - Treatment: Mantel–Haenszel ratios on strata of place × time × visible cue stay within |ln ratio| ≤ 0.05 (a proposed margin). A planted 1.25× evening stop bias read 1.075 over all periods but 1.269 within evening and night strata, so report by period too (measured here).
   - Twins: compare against a single-culture world, a culture-blind twin with preference shifts set to zero, and customs counterfactuals such as all festivals moved to daytime.
   - Use agent-level units. A person-day chi-square flagged clean code falsely, because offending clusters within low-income agents (measured here).

### What culture-blind rules miss

- **Place.** In a toy town with culture-blind policing, putting 80% of three minorities in hot districts gave 3.15× stops and 8.18× arrests per person (p = 0.005, measured here). Housing therefore never reads culture.
- **Price.** In the same toy, favourite-food prices of 7.00–13.00 against an equal 9.00 gave 7.0× offending, 8.1× arrest and 13.3× wealth gaps (measured here). In the toy, people met their basic food need with their culture's favourite food, so its price set their hunger. Section 2's design rules this out: subsistence is bought cheapest-first whatever the culture, and total spending and saving never read culture (inference). Favoured categories also start at equal base prices.
- **Exposure.** A culture with evening festivals was victimised 8.7% more than with daytime festivals [7.9–9.5%]. Against the daytime culture, its per-person gap was 11.0% [8.1–14.0%]. Its rate ratio per outdoor hour at the same cell, tick and visible cue was 1.019 [0.991–1.048]. Exposure explains about 81% of that per-person gap (measured here, 50 paired seeds × 10,000 agents). Equal festival timing removes this by construction; the lesson that time and place drive victimisation is taught by place and hour, never by culture (see (o)).
- **Networks.** If festival friendships ever carried job referrals, culture could reach employment, as Calvó-Armengol and Jackson show for real groups (search summary). Keep festival contact transient unless an employment-by-culture audit also runs.
- **Weak random draws.** An ad-hoc hash mixer moved the audit's ratio for the smaller culture from 1.047 to 0.950 between seed batches. A murmur3-style finaliser after every input gave 1.027 and 1.017 (measured here). The keyed draw needs full avalanche after every input, which matters well beyond culture.
- **Hue, wealth and text.** Proposed checks, with unsourced thresholds:
  - Cramér's V < 0.05 between culture and wealth decile, home district and job at spawn (culture ids have no order, so a rank correlation does not apply);
  - a χ² test of hue × culture over 10⁶ births;
  - a culture row in the appearance audit, with |Cramér's V| < 0.05 for every rendered attribute outside the lens;
  - the culture dissimilarity index across districts, flagged above 0.2;
  - lints that reject bare-plural generic sentences and hierarchy words in culture strings;
  - a diverse playtest panel, of whom at least 8 in 10 name no real people a culture resembles and see no difference in crime.

## Conflicts between the notes, resolved

Each item states the decision and why. Items (a)–(j) come from the transmission and customs notes; (k)–(s) from setting them against the ethics note.

**(a) One per-person layout: 6 bytes.** The transmission note stores a culture plus five 4-bit custom nibbles (5 B). The customs note stores `culture`, `culture2`, a domain-mix byte, `birthCulture` and a Uint16 `homeRegion` (6 B). Decision:

- `culture` (Uint8): the current culture, which the identity rule may move.
- `birthCulture` (Uint8): fixed at birth by the lead-parent rule. It sets the name's structure and serves as the inherited label.
- `customs` (Uint16): four 4-bit nibbles for food, festival, music and naming, each the culture whose version the person holds. The nibbles generalise `culture2` and the mix byte, since each custom may come from any culture.
- `homeRegion` (Uint16): the birth settlement's region, never changed.

Why: the transmission note's fifth custom, home region, passes vertically only, so the birth culture already carries it, and a region id records where a person was born. The total is 6 B (computed).

**(b) At most 8 cultures per world.** The nibbles allow 16, but round 6 used an eight-class map palette, and the customs note proposes 4–8 hearths. Decision: 4–8 cultures per world; the nibbles keep headroom.

**(c) The settlement block keeps mixed counts.** The transmission note's block holds counts by primary culture plus mixed counts, 2K Int32. The customs note's holds 8 counts and a region id. Decision: 2K Int32 plus the region id, 66 B. Why: spawn needs the mixed counts to give "mixed" people local customs, and the block still fits 1 KB. The save cost is (e).

**(d) Keyed stochastic rounding for every split of people.** The transmission note uses keyed systematic rounding and found that plain flooring silenced small villages. The customs note found that plain largest remainder never moves minorities in small flows. Decision: split people flows by whole parts first, then give each leftover person a culture by a keyed draw weighted by the remainders. Largest remainder stays only for zero-sum share tables such as the household's preference shifts.

**(e) Save size is a range until M6 measures it.** Dense counts with mixed counts saved at 117,730 B gzip for 10,000 settlements; 8 counts saved at 53,367 B dense and 50,116 B top-3 sparse (measured here, on different synthetic countries). Decision: plan on 50–118 KB, use the sparse layout, and measure the full country save.

**(f) Cost neutrality comes from the budget rule, with equal base prices as a backstop.** The transmission note asks that each culture's favourite foods cost the same at base prices. The customs note keeps welfare fixed by moving only discretionary shares, and shows local price gaps rather than correcting them. The ethics note asks for swaps within the budget and one equal festival budget, asserted in a unit test. Decision: all three. Local premia then emerge from markets and appear in headless reports.

**(g) Hearths and borders.** The transmission note places hearths by farthest-point sampling and mixes frontier towns by share₁ = 1 − ½(d₁/d₂)². The customs note uses keyed Poisson-disc hearths and a border band of width d_b. Decision: keyed Poisson-disc hearths and the border band, which keeps region cores uniform; the 50–100-year ledger spin-up then mixes the interior through migration (inference). Both formulas give a 50 : 50 mix on the border itself.

**(h) One guard suite.** The transmission note's "decision swap" is the customs note's "flip test", and its group monitors against a culture-blind twin are the customs note's exposure audit. The ethics note's outcome audit and single-culture twin join them. Decision: section 4's four layers, with the flip test also comparing draw keys, because a culture-keyed draw changed 231 decisions and no thresholds in the transmission prototype.

**(i) Identity: store both, show the inherited label.** The transmission note lets identity follow practice with a lag; the customs note keeps a birth culture; the ethics note asks that switching be gradual and keep the person's name. Decision: store both, as in (a); the inspector and the culture lens show the inherited label, as the owner chose. Civilization VII's reversal suggests players dislike identity changes they did not choose (inference).

**(j) Corrections to earlier rounds and rules.**

- Round 4 proposed fused English site suffixes such as "-ford" and "-haven" for place names. Fused suffixes would make every culture read English, so site words become separate UI-language words.
- The content rule's place-name filter, edit distance 2, rejects about 42% of 3- and 4-letter names. The owner approved the refined rule in section 2, which changes that content rule.
- Round 1's Schelling known-answer test stays on neutral colours only.
- The plan's keyed draw needs a full-avalanche finaliser after every input, and its χ² test should cover cross-stream pairs.

**(k) Names: one shared sound set, adopted.** The customs note designs one phonology per culture, screened against real languages. The ethics note asks for one shared phonology, with naming customs setting only structure. Recommendation: one shared sound set. Why: per-culture sounds make a name reveal culture wherever it appears, which feeds illusory correlation; 6 of the customs note's 9 example designs scored at or above its 0.26 review bar; and a per-region approach proved "too hard to make it clear" for O'Leary. Per-culture sound sets remain an option for place and festival names after the screen and the panel. It trades flavour for safety, and the owner accepted it.

**(l) Partner choice: shared customs or blind.** The transmission prototype makes partners prefer their own culture, more strongly the more of their own customs they keep (0.2 plus 0.1 per own custom kept, applied by both partners). The ethics note asks that partner choice never read culture. Recommendation: weight partner candidates by how many customs they share, a preference that reads customs rather than the culture label, calibrated to the prototype's exogamy bands (inference; untested). Why: partner choice is not a judgement, homogamy is the best-evidenced brake on heritage loss, and culture is spawned independently of wealth and home, so homogamy has no culture–wealth link to preserve; the dissimilarity, wealth and outcome audits watch for one. Without it, cultures would blend faster; no run tested blind partner choice (inference). The owner accepted this recommendation.

**(m) Two audit bands, two roles.** The ethics note proposes raw per-culture outcomes within 0.9–1.1, for the 90% interval of the ratio. The customs note proposes fine-strata equivalence within |ln ratio| ≤ 0.05. Decision: both. The raw band checks that no culture ends up worse off overall; the fine strata check that no rule treats cultures differently. With equal festival timing (n), the raw band is expected to hold (inference).

**(n) Festival timing: equal by construction, adopted.** The customs note lets each culture's festivals have their own timing and shows the resulting exposure honestly. The ethics note asks for equal exposure and leaves the choice to the owner. Recommendation: every culture gets the same mix of daytime and evening festival days, spread across the year; lab cards may break the parity on purpose. Why: in the toy, evening festivals alone raised one culture's victimisation by 8.7%, and any such gap shown beside a culture label risks building a stereotype. The owner accepted it.

**(o) The exposure lesson is taught by place and hour, never by culture.** The customs note proposes an opt-in Exposure lens and a bet card ("Evening festivals: will that culture be stopped more?") that show victimisation and police contact per culture. The ethics note's evidence says pairing a group label with crime events builds stereotypes even at equal rates. Decision: the Exposure lens shows night outdoor hours, and victimisation and police contacts per outdoor hour, by district and hour, and the bet card asks "Evening events: are people out at night stopped more?". Per-culture audit numbers stay in headless CI reports, and the "What this toy leaves out" page publishes the audit result in words.

**(p) Patrols never read the festival calendar.** The customs note suggests calendar-aware patrols as a place-and-time remedy. The ethics note bars patrols from reading culture or festival crowds. Decision: patrols never read the festival calendar, because festivals belong to cultures and patrol allocation is a guarded decision. Remedies are lighting and lab sliders.

**(q) Similar culture shares, no host culture.** The transmission prototype started from one 60% culture and seven smaller ones, to match migrant data. The ethics note asks for similar shares, because illusory correlation needs a distinctive minority. Decision: presets give cultures similar country-wide shares and name no default, host or majority culture; local dominance near hearths is natural. The 60% runs stay as calibration scenarios, and the UI never says "host", "native" or "foreign". The transmission bands and the 0.5% inflow default came from those runs, so they should be re-run with similar shares before CI fixes them (inference).

**(r) Home regions must not line up with development.** The ethics note asks that each culture span different development levels and that every region hold at least two cultures. The hearth design in (g) does not guarantee that. Decision: reject hearth layouts whose cultures differ in mean land quality or development beyond a set tolerance (a proposal), and audit culture against settlement wealth bands. The spin-up should give most regions two or more cultures (inference).

**(s) Customs and records never share a screen.** The customs note shows names in the inspector and follow-cam and keeps them out of justice views. The ethics note adds separate Customs and Records tabs and a culture lens that hides justice cues. Decision: both, as in section 3.
