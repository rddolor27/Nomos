# Pixel-art asset packs for a Pokémon-style top-down town simulation: contents, scale and exact licence terms (status October 2026)

## Which packs cover the needed pieces (town exteriors, 4-direction walking characters for citizens/police/merchants/thieves, emotes and icons, UI, a modular character generator)?

### Takeaway
No single free pack covers everything. Two CC0 sources can be committed to an MIT repo with no conditions and together cover most needs:
- **Ninja Adventure** (CC0, 16×16): about 90 characters with 4-direction × 4-frame walks, 30 emote bubbles, dialog-box UI, and village/market tiles.
- **Kenney** (CC0, 16×16): modern roads, shops and cars from Roguelike Modern City and RPG Urban Pack, plus an emote set that includes a "$" cash bubble.

No CC0 pack found has a police station, a bank, a jail, a police officer or handcuff icons. Those are covered only by paid packs: LimeZu Modern Exteriors has a Police Station theme, Modern Interiors has a Jail theme and a Character Generator, and maru-98 sells a Police Characters Pack. Otherwise the developer has to recolour or draw them.

### Inferences
- The minimum set that covers the roles is:
  - Ninja Adventure for citizens: Villager1–6, Woman, OldMan and OldWoman each have full 4-direction walks.
  - Kenney Emotes for "$" and anger, plus Ninja Adventure emotes for "!", "?", hearts and "Zz".
  - Kenney Roguelike City or RPG Urban for roads, a shop with an awning, cars and a park.
  - A Kenney police car for the police.
- What still needs work: a police officer sprite (recolour a villager blue and add a cap, or use the "Inspector" / PIPOYA "Soldier" as a stand-in), a merchant (add an apron), a thief overlay (a 16×16 mask/bandana layer drawn over any citizen, which fits "thief is a temporary state"), a bank and a jail. CC0 allows all of these edits. The jail could reuse Tiny Dungeon grates or Ninja Adventure's iron fence.
- Handcuff or jail-bar icons were not found in any CC0 pack. Kenney's `emote_bars` (vertical bars) or a cross emote could stand in, or a custom 16×16 icon could be drawn.
- For a modern Pokémon-town feel with an actual police station and jail, LimeZu Exteriors + Interiors is the only cohesive option found. It is paid and its files cannot be redistributed.
- Kenney's 1-Bit Pack and Micro Roguelike (8×8) are stylistically far from a GBA/DS look. They suit a minimalist alternative, not the target look.

## What exactly do the licences say about commercial use, committing raw files to a public open-source repo, modification, attribution, share-alike scope, and AI/NFT/resale restrictions?

### Takeaway
- **CC0** (Ninja Adventure, Kenney, CC0-only LPC files) allows everything, including committing the raw files to a public MIT repo, with no credit required.
- **CC-BY / OGA-BY / CC-BY-SA / GPL** (most LPC art, most Tuxemon art) allows commercial use and public redistribution if credits ship with the game. Share-alike or GPL applies to the art and art derived from it, not, per the generator's own text and OpenGameArt forum consensus, to separate MIT code.
- **Every paid or "free but restricted" pack checked** forbids redistributing the files, so their raw files should not be committed to a public repo: LimeZu, Cozy People, Sprout Lands, Tiny Swords (current version), Cainos, PIPOYA, Mana Seed and Time Fantasy.
- **AI and NFT clauses**: LimeZu (no AI training; no NFTs on GameDev Market), Sprout Lands ("No NFT's"), and most strictly Mana Seed (no NFT/Web3/GenAI projects, and reportedly no use alongside any AI-generated assets, including code).

### Inferences
- **CC0 art in an MIT repo.** This is the simplest case: the files can be committed. It is still good practice to keep each pack's `LICENSE.txt` beside the files in an `assets/` folder, and to state in the README that the art is CC0, not MIT. Kenney's logo is not covered.
- **CC-BY / OGA-BY / CC-BY-SA art in an MIT repo.**
  - The generator's wording ("derivative artwork or modifications") scopes share-alike to the art, and the OGA forum summary agrees that code is not affected.
  - So MIT code can sit beside CC-BY-SA art, provided the art keeps its own licence and a CREDITS file ships with the deployed game.
  - Where a file is multi-licensed, choosing its OGA-BY or CC-BY option avoids share-alike and GPL entirely. 11,868 of the 13,915 ULPC credit rows offer one.
  - The CC-BY/CC-BY-SA "may not encrypt or protect" clause does not obviously bite a static, DRM-free web build.
  - This is how the licences are stated, not legal advice.
- **GPL-only art** (some LPC files) is the murkiest to combine with an MIT codebase. Avoiding copyleft-only files with the generator's licence filter is the conservative choice.
- **Paid packs and a public repo.** Every paid pack examined forbids redistribution. Committing their raw PNGs to a public GitHub repo would make them freely downloadable by third parties. By the logic of PIPOYA's clarification ("if a third party cannot use the asset directly, it is not considered redistribution"), that would count as redistribution. They therefore belong in a private submodule or private storage, pulled in at build time, with only the built site published. Whether serving a deployed browser game that necessarily exposes the PNGs counts as normal "use in a project" is not addressed in the snippets. It is standard practice for web games, but it is unverified.
- **Mana Seed's AI clause.** As summarised, it would rule out using it in a codebase that contains AI-generated code (for example code written with Claude Code), so Mana Seed should be excluded unless that wording is checked directly.
- **LimeZu's "no AI training" clause** means LimeZu art should not be fed to an AI generator as style input.
- **Tuxemon's claim that LimeZu packs are "CC BY"** reflects an older listing (2020–2021 archive links). Current LimeZu terms should be treated as authoritative, and LimeZu art should not be lifted from Tuxemon's repo.

### Gaps
- Exact current licence text could not be opened for any itch.io-hosted pack, for kenney.nl, LimeZu, the Mana Seed licence page, timefantasy.net or pipoya.net (all blocked). Wording marked "snippet only" may be paraphrased by the search engine and should be re-checked before the developer relies on it.
- OGA-BY 3.0 full text could not be opened (static.opengameart.org blocked).
- Whether LimeZu's current terms allow a deployed open-source web build to ship its PNGs, and how the "credit" must be displayed, were not found.
- Mana Seed's precise AI wording (whether "AI-generated … code" is meant literally) is only known from a search summary.

## Which packs share a compatible scale and palette so they can be mixed?

### Takeaway
There are two scale families:
- **16-px world**: Kenney (all the "Tiny", RPG Urban, Roguelike City and Emotes packs), Ninja Adventure, LimeZu at its 16-px size, Time Fantasy, Mana Seed tiles, maru-98 police, and the Pokémon-like 16×16 packs.
- **32-px world**: LPC/ULPC with 64×64 frames, PIPOYA, Cainos, and LPC Revised.

Within the 16-px family, Kenney Tiny Town + Tiny Dungeon are designed to match each other. Ninja Adventure characters and emotes are 1 tile tall and can sit on Kenney 16×16 tiles. Palettes differ between authors, so a recolour or palette-lock pass is needed.

### Cited Findings
- **Kenney**:
  - Tiny Town, Tiny Dungeon, RPG Urban, Roguelike City, Emote Pack and Monochrome RPG are all 16×16; Micro Roguelike is 8×8 — [shorepine index, opened](https://github.com/shorepine/kenney/blob/main/index.tsv)
  - "The Tiny Town pack perfectly fits the previous Tiny Dungeon release" — [X, snippet only](https://x.com/KenneyNL/status/1613512551119167492)
  - RPG Urban Pack's packed tilemap is a uniform 16-px grid (27×18) with characters inside single 16×16 cells; Roguelike City tiles are 16×16 with a 1 px margin — [RPG Urban, opened](https://github.com/ETdoFresh/kenney.nl/blob/master/kenney_rpgurbanpack/Tilemap/tilemap.txt); [Roguelike City, opened](https://github.com/ETdoFresh/kenney.nl/blob/master/roguelike-modern-city-pack/Spritesheet/spritesheetInfo.txt)
- **Ninja Adventure**: characters are 16×16 per frame (sheets of 64×112 and 64×64); tilesets sit on a 16-px grid (528×368 = 33×23 tiles); emotes are 14×13 — [opened](https://github.com/rinn7e/ninja-adventure-indigo/tree/master/assets/Ninja%20Adventure%20-%20Asset%20Pack)
- **LimeZu**: Exteriors, Interiors and the Character Generator each come in 16×16, 32×32 and 48×48 versions — [itch, snippet only](https://limezu.itch.io/modernexteriors)
- **Mana Seed**: "These tilesets use 16x16 tiles and the proportions are designed for character sprites around 32 pixels high", in 64×64 cells — [itch, snippet only](https://seliel-the-shaper.itch.io/character-base)
- **Time Fantasy**: "based on a 16x16 grid" — [itch bundle, snippet only](https://itch.io/s/55012/time-fantasy-rpg-asset-bundle)
- **Cozy People**: "32x32 px layout" — [itch, snippet only](https://shubibubi.itch.io/cozy-people)
- **LPC**: original LPC "stuck to a standard 64x64 format" for character frames — [ULPC README, opened](https://github.com/LiberatedPixelCup/Universal-LPC-Spritesheet-Character-Generator/blob/master/README.md). LPC Revised uses "a 32-pixel style" and "approximately 170 colors" — [opened](https://github.com/ElizaWy/LPC/blob/main/README.md)
- **PIPOYA**: 32×32 frames (96×128 sheets) — [opened](https://github.com/pythonarcade/community-rpg/tree/main/resources/characters). **Cainos**: "32x32" — [snippet only](https://cainos.itch.io/pixel-art-top-down-basic)
- **maru-98 police** and the **Pokémon-like packs** (maciaz, Pocket Creature Tamer) are all 16×16 — [maru-98, snippet only](https://maru-98.itch.io/police-characters-pack-16x16-pixel-art-top-down); [maciaz, snippet only](https://maciaz.itch.io/pokemon-like-top-down-tile-set)

### Inferences
- **Proportions.** GBA-era Pokémon overworlds (background knowledge, not sourced here) use 16-px tiles with characters about one tile wide and somewhat taller than one tile. Mana Seed (~32 px tall characters on 16×16 tiles) and probably LimeZu are closest to those proportions. Ninja Adventure and Kenney characters are chibi, one tile tall, so they read as "cute Zelda-like" rather than "Pokémon trainer". The upside of the chibi size is that 10k agents at 16×16 stay legible when zoomed out.
- **Palettes, from visual inspection.**
  - Ninja Adventure: warm and saturated with dark outlines.
  - Kenney Roguelike City: cooler and greyer.
  - Kenney RPG Urban: brighter, with lavender-grey roads.
  - Kenney Tiny Town: outlined, earthy.
  - Mixing Ninja Adventure characters with Kenney city tiles works in scale but will likely need a shared palette (re-index both to one palette) for a coherent look.
- **Emotes.** Kenney's white emote bubbles are neutral enough to sit over any 16-px character.
- **Mixing the two families.** Combining 32-px packs (LPC, PIPOYA, Cainos) with 16-px packs means either 2× nearest-neighbour upscaling of the 16-px set or downscaling the 32-px art. Downscaling pixel art usually destroys it, so in practice you pick one family.

### Gaps
- LimeZu, Time Fantasy and Sprout Lands frame dimensions, and Tiny Swords' tile size, were not verified because itch.io is blocked.
- Palette comparisons are my visual judgement from the opened sheets. No palette files were compared numerically.

## What do AI pixel-art generators (PixelLab, Retro Diffusion) offer in 2026, and what do their terms say about ownership and commercial use?

### Takeaway
Both tools now generate game-ready sprites with 4- or 8-direction rotations, walk/idle animations and tilesets. Both say the user owns the output and may use it commercially.
- PixelLab adds a ban on using outputs to train other models.
- Retro Diffusion is pay-per-image; PixelLab is subscription-based.

Using either conflicts with Mana Seed's licence as summarised, and should not involve LimeZu art as input, because of LimeZu's no-AI-training clause.

### Cited Findings
- **PixelLab terms**:
  - "You own the copyrights to your creations, permitting usage for both commercial and non-commercial purposes"; you are "free to use, modify, and distribute the outputs … for any purpose, except for training other models without their explicit permission"; the user bears "full legal responsibility" that content does not infringe third-party rights — [PixelLab Terms of Service, snippet only](https://www.pixellab.ai/termsofservice); [PixelLab FAQ, snippet only](https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/faq)
- **PixelLab features and pricing (third-party summaries)**:
  - "skeleton-based character animation with 4-direction and 8-direction rotation, native tileset generation … an Aseprite plugin plus a documented API"
  - Free trial of 40 generations; Pixel Apprentice $12/month (up to 2,000 generations); Pixel Artisan $24/month; Pixel Architect $50/month (10,000 generations)

  — [righterofwords.com 2026 roundup, snippet only](https://righterofwords.com/2026/09/10/the-7-best-pixel-art-generators-for-games-and-merchandise-in-2026/); [cinevva guide, snippet only](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-pixel-art-generators); [PixelLab API page, snippet only](https://www.pixellab.ai/pixellab-api)
- **Retro Diffusion terms**: "all pixel art generated with Retro Diffusion is yours to use for personal or commercial projects, including games and other creative works" — [retrodiffusion.ai FAQ, snippet only](https://retrodiffusion.ai/)
- **Retro Diffusion features and pricing**:
  - The API can "constrain output to a palette image, generate tiles that repeat seamlessly on either axis"; animation styles "include walking, idle, jump and attack battle sprites, an 8-direction rotation, and VFX, returned as a GIF or a PNG sprite sheet"; it is also offered as a hosted MCP server with "90+ pixel art styles".
  - Pricing is pay-per-generation: about $0.015 (RD Fast) to $0.18 per image (RD Pro); animations $0.07–$0.25; tilesets $0.10; credits never expire.
  - New accounts get 50 free credits, and credit packs start at $5. There is also an Aseprite extension.

  — [Retro Diffusion MCP listing, snippet only](https://mcpservers.org/servers/retro-diffusion/retro-diffusion-mcp); [retrodiffusion.ai, snippet only](https://retrodiffusion.ai/); [Aseprite extension on itch, snippet only](https://astropulse.itch.io/retrodiffusion)

### Inferences
- **Fit for this project.** A palette-locked generator (Retro Diffusion's palette constraint) or PixelLab's 4-direction rotation could produce the missing police, merchant and masked-thief variants at 16 px to match a chosen CC0 base. The output would still need hand cleanup to match the base pack's outline style.
- **Generated art in a public MIT repo.** By both vendors' terms, commercial use and redistribution are allowed. PixelLab's "no training other models" restriction travels with the outputs, which matters if the repo is public and others might scrape it. That is a limit on the user's own conduct and does not obviously require any notice.

### Gaps
- Vendor pages could not be opened (pixellab.ai and retrodiffusion.ai are blocked). Pricing comes from third-party roundups and may be out of date.
- Whether AI-generated images are protected by copyright at all (which affects "you own the copyright") depends on jurisdiction and was not researched. This is not legal advice.

## Recommended primary combination, paid upgrade path, and make-your-own path

### Takeaway
- **Primary (free, all CC0, safe to commit to the MIT repo)**:
  - Ninja Adventure for characters, walks, emotes, dialog UI and market/village props.
  - Kenney Roguelike Modern City + RPG Urban Pack for roads, shops, cars and a park.
  - The Kenney Emotes Pack for the "$" cash bubble, anger and alert.
  - The Kenney Pixel Vehicle Pack police car.
  - Recolour and overlay edits for police, merchant and the thief mask.
- **Paid upgrade (about $6.50, plus about €4 if needed)**: LimeZu Modern Exteriors ($5, Police Station theme) + Modern Interiors (≥$1.50, Character Generator and Jail theme), optionally maru-98's Police Characters (€3.99). Keep these raw files out of the public repo and credit LimeZu.
- **Make-your-own**: a 16-px layered paper-doll built from CC0 bases. Prototype with ULPC filtered to CC0/OGA-BY/CC-BY if 32-px art is acceptable, or use PixelLab or Retro Diffusion for the missing variants, avoiding Mana Seed and LimeZu-as-input.

### Inferences
- **Primary combination, concretely.**
  - Tiles: Kenney Roguelike Modern City (roads, crosswalks, parking, grocery store with awning as the "market", glass and brick buildings to label as "bank" and "police station" with a sign or recolour, park) plus Kenney RPG Urban (pools, benches, lamps).
  - Market stalls: Ninja Adventure TilesetHouse (awnings, crates of goods).
  - Jail: Ninja Adventure's iron fence or Tiny Dungeon grates.
  - Agents: Ninja Adventure Villager1–6, Woman, OldMan and OldWoman as citizens (4-direction × 4-frame walk). Police: a blue recolour of a villager with a cap, or "Inspector". Merchants: a villager with an added apron.
  - Thief: a runtime overlay layer (mask/bandana) applied while the agent is in the "thief" state, which matches crime being a temporary action rather than a costume.
  - Emotes: Kenney cash "$", anger, "!" and "?", plus Ninja Adventure "Zz", hearts and faces. Ninja Adventure dialog boxes for the UI.
  - Everything is CC0. Commit `LICENSE.txt` files beside the assets and note "art: CC0" in the README.
- **Style risk.** Ninja Adventure has a Japanese-fantasy flavour and the Kenney city is modern. If the mix looks off, either go all-Kenney (RPG Urban's 6 walking characters plus recolours) or all-Ninja-Adventure (village look plus a drawn police station and bank) before buying LimeZu.
- **Paid path.** LimeZu gives the most "modern Pokémon town" coherence: Police Station, Jail, a character generator that can make officers and shopkeepers, and 16/32/48 sizes. The cost is a private-asset build pipeline (submodule or private bucket), a visible "LimeZu" credit, and no AI training on the art.
- **Make-your-own path.**
  - Define a 16×16 (or 16×24) base body with 4 directions × 3–4 frames.
  - Add layered outfit sheets (citizen colours, police uniform and cap, merchant apron, thief mask) composited at load time into one atlas, with palette swaps for variety. That keeps a single texture atlas for 10k agents.
  - Seed it from CC0 Ninja Adventure or Kenney sprites, since CC0 allows derivatives with no attribution.
  - Use PixelLab or Retro Diffusion only to draft variants, then hand-clean them.
  - Do not combine with Mana Seed. Do not feed LimeZu art to the generators.

### Gaps
- No CC0 pack with an explicit police station, bank, jail building, police officer or handcuff icon was found. These must be bought (LimeZu, maru-98) or made.
- The visual compatibility of Ninja Adventure characters on Kenney city tiles has not been tested in an actual mockup. The previews folder provides the files for that test.
